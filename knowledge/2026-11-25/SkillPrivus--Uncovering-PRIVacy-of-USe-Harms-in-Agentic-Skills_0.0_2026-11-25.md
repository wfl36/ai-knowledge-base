## 项目描述
arXiv:2611.26747v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Agent skills extend AI assistants through installable instructions and code that can access users' files, accounts, and data. Even when performing their advertised tasks, skills may infer sensitive attributes, retain unnecessary information, or disclose data to unexpected recipients. We study these potential privacy-of-use harms through SkillPrivus, a static audit framework that analyzes skills before installation without executing them. SkillPrivus extracts data operations from code and natural-language instructions, grounds them in source evidence, and adapts Contextual Integrity to assess their appropriateness. Applied to 726 real skills from public marketplaces, 563 of which handle personal data, SkillPrivus flags at least one potential violation in 307 of these skills (54.5%), and its judgments agree with three blind human annotators on 83.2% of resolvable moves. The flagged behaviors include sensitive inferences, information use beyond the stated task, and disclosures to unexpected recipients, suggesting that privacy harms can arise from ordinary skill functionality.
