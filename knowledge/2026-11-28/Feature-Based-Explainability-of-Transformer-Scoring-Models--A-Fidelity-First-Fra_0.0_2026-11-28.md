## 项目描述
arXiv:2611.33175v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Transformer-based automated writing scoring models can achieve strong agreement with human scores while providing limited transparency into the response characteristics associated with their predictions. This study evaluated a feature-based post-hoc explanation approach in which explanatory fidelity was established before feature importance was interpreted. English Language Arts responses from three writing prompts in Grades 6 and 8 were analyzed, with about 12,700 responses per prompt and separate DeBERTa-v3-base research models for Expression and Conventions. Each response was represented by 61 interpretable linguistic and writing-quality features. Four approaches - theory-driven composites, hierarchical feature groupings, exploratory factor analysis, and Random Forest surrogates - were compared in their ability to reproduce DeBERTa predictions. Random Forest surrogates produced the largest observed R2 values across all six prompt-by-trait conditions, with held-out R2 values from .81 to .95. SHAP analyses showed substantial overlap in high-importance features across traits, with 75% to 90% of the top 20 features shared. Trait differentiation occurred mainly through relative feature weighting: Expression showed greater importance for lexical and conceptual indicators, whereas Conventions showed greater importance for sentence-level and syntactic-control indicators. Under researcher-defined mappings, rubric correspondence was 90%-95% for Expression and 12%-18% for Conventions. These percentages are descriptive and conditional on the mappings and available linguistic features. The findings support a fidelity-first framework for examining apparent scoring priorities while distinguishing post-hoc evidence about model behavior from direct explanation of transformer internals.
