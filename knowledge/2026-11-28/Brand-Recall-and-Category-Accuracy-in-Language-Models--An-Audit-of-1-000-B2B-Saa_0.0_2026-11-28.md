## 项目描述
arXiv:2611.33322v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A language model can name a brand without classifying it correctly. This study measures these outcomes separately for 1,000 B2B SaaS brands across 333 product categories. On Gemma 4 12B, with no external retrieval, category prompts elicit 510 brands (51.0%). A separate five-candidate category probe ranks the reference label first for 787 brands, a distractor first for 194, and yields little separation for 19. Among the 981 separated rankings, the error rate is 19.8%. It falls to 3.5% for the 547 brands with a winner-to-runner-up log-probability margin above 0.6. These are within-cohort measurements, not calibrated confidence estimates. Exploratory regressions associate technical coverage with recall and an owned-properties composite with category accuracy, but the composites use different adjustment sets and do not establish causal effects. Across four Gemma generations, measured recall is 39.8%, 44.3%, 51.1% and 51.0%. A separate representation-based sentiment measure has little linear association with customer ratings (r=0.03, n=921). The findings support reporting recall and category accuracy separately. Failure to elicit a brand does not establish its absence from model parameters or training data.
