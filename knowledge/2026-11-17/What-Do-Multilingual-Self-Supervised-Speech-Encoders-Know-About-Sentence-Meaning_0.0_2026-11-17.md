## 项目描述
arXiv:2611.17686v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Self-supervised speech models (S3Ms) have been scaled up to thousands of languages and millions of hours of audio, yet little is known about how these models structure semantic knowledge across languages. In this work, we probe sentence-level semantics in massively multilingual S3Ms across 76 languages, using linear maps from frozen speech representations into a frozen text embedding space, evaluated by cross-modal retrieval. Sentence meaning is linearly accessible but highly uneven across languages and resource tiers. Cross-lingual probes can match language-specific probes on languages never seen in probe training, but per-model monolingual decodability does not predict transfer. Within the OmniASR wav2vec family, scaling from 300M--7B disproportionately benefits high-resource languages, and ASR tuning sharply improves semantic decodability while making representations more language-specific. However, semantic content is entangled with acoustics, inflating apparent performance on single-speaker corpora.
