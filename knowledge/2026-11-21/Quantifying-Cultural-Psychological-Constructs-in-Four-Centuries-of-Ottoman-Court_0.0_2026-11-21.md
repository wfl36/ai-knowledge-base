## 项目描述
arXiv:2611.24981v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Populations vary along cultural dimensions such as individualism, collectivism, and honor. But how can these dimensions be studied in historical societies for which conventional psychological data are unavailable? Here, we introduce a computational text-analytic framework for quantifying psychological constructs in 54,670 Ottoman Turkish court records from 21 Istanbul courts spanning 1513-1911 CE. We develop sentence-embedding models adapted to Ottoman legal language and extend a theory-driven, text-analytic pipeline to capture cultural variation along the dimensions of individualism, collectivism, and honor in this historical corpus. We evaluate our models through multiple methods: document retrieval, semantic textual similarity, questionnaire-item classification, and validation against expert annotations. Our adapted sentence transformer (OTA-BERTurk) achieved a Recall@1 of .843 on the test set for document retrieval, a Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) of .806 for semantic textual similarity (meaning the best answer usually appeared as a top result), and classified questionnaire items with 89.58\% accuracy. Document-level performance varied across constructs and methods. Qualitative analysis further showed that high scores reflected historically specific legal and institutional patterns. We publicly release the models, construct representations, scoring pipeline, as well as an interactive web interface to support transparent, reproducible historical-psychological analyses of Ottoman Turkish court records spanning approximately four centuries. These computational tools enable the study of psychological variation across centuries of cultural and institutional change in a diverse empire.
