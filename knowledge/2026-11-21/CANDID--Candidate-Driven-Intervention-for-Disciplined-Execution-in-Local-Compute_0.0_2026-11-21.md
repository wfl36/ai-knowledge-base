## 项目描述
arXiv:2611.24977v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We present CANDID (Candidate-Driven Intervention for Disciplined Execution), an execution-control framework designed to enhance the reliability of local computer-use agents (CUAs) in resource-constrained settings. While local CUAs feature robust baseline proficiency in single-step action prediction, they frequently fail at critical junctions due to rigid action loops or premature DONE decisions. Rather than imposing imprecise cognitive loads through textual reflection, CANDID repurposes parallel test-time scaling into a disciplined, execution-level intervention at sparse, failure-prone points. We introduce a loop-recovery mechanism to filter candidates away from repeated behaviors and a conservative, unanimous DONE gating policy that requires absolute consensus to terminate. Experiments on OSWorld demonstrate that CANDID's structural control significantly improves success rates, outperforming feedback-based alternatives with minimal execution overhead.
